# DAK conformance — self-monitoring of blood pressure in pregnancy

What these forms take from the WHO SMBP DAK, what they deliberately do differently, and what the
DAK specifies that a Tamanu form cannot hold.

## Source

Everything is generated by [`../dak_generator.py`](../dak_generator.py) from the kit's **core data
dictionary** (`SMBP DAK_core data dictionary.xlsx`, 122 labelled rows across the two process tabs
of the five the workbook holds). It gives each data element an ID (`SMBP.B4.DE.4`), a label, a
data type, an `Optionality` flag, terminology codes (ICD-11 / ICD-10 / LOINC / SNOMED CT / ICHI /
ICF / ATC) and — for coded elements — one child row per value.

The guidance prompts come from the kit's **decision-support logic**
(`SMBP DAK_decision-support logic.xlsx`, three decision tables). Nothing is authored by hand.

**This kit is not published as IRIS Web Annexes.** WHO ships its components through the SMART
guidelines implementation guide at [smart.who.int/dak-smbp](https://smart.who.int/dak-smbp/),
whose component pages serve whatever the site last built, so [`domain.py`](domain.py) pins each
component to WHO's own source repository for that guide —
`WorldHealthOrganization/smart-dak-smbp` — at the **`v1.0.0` tag**, whose `sushi-config.yaml`
declares `status: active` and `releaseLabel: release`. That is a version-pinned path to the
released bytes, which the component page is not; the component page is recorded alongside it as
`page`.

## What the forms are

Two surveys: 42 dictionary questions across 2 screens, 53 option strings, 22 mandatory, and 10
Annex B rows — seven guidance prompts and three patient issues. The spec holds 54 rows in all, the
rest being screen headings. See the [README](README.md) for the table.

## One activity, one screen

This is the kit's structural signature, and it is the opposite of the IMMZ kit's.

Every other DAK divides a process tab into activities, and the reader turns each activity into a
screen: the activity id changes, a heading is written, and the questions beneath it start a page.
The IMMZ kit pushes that the other way and packs nine activities onto one tab, which is why
`PROCESSES` can take a slice of a tab by activity.

This kit gives each tab **exactly one activity**. All 72 rows of `SMBP.A Identification & prep`
are `SMBP.A9 Download and set up personal health tracking`; all 50 rows of `SMBP.Conduct SMBP` are
`SMBP.B4 Input result into personal health tracking application`. It heads no sections either, so
there is no sub-structure of any kind for the reader to find, and each survey is a single screen.

No section headings are invented to break them up. A screen division is a statement about which
questions belong together, and the kit makes none; the questions are in the dictionary's own row
order, which puts the client's history before her medications before her baseline, and the reading
form's context block after the reading that raises it.

## Three activities, two tabs

The kit's business processes name a third activity the dictionary has no rows for. `SMBP.B7 Review
health actions based on SMBP results` is where two of the three decision tables sit —
`SMBP.B7.DT.01 SMBP health actions` and `SMBP.B7.DT.02 Danger signs` — and it collects nothing:
the whole of it is reading what the client has already recorded and telling her what to do about
it.

So there is no third survey. What that activity produces is guidance, and guidance is carried as
the Annex B prompts on the reading form, where the answers that raise it are.

## What the patient record keeps

Seven elements of the set-up tab, `SMBP.A9.DE.1` through `DE.7`, are not on the form because
Tamanu already holds them: the client's unique identification, first name, last name, date of
birth, address, telephone number and email address.

A DAK registers its own client because it cannot assume a record exists. Tamanu has one, and
collecting a name or a telephone number onto a form as well gives a deployment two places to write
one fact and no rule for which counts.

Twelve further elements — `SMBP.A9.DE.20` through `DE.31`, the closest facility's name, phone and
address, and the same three for the client's health worker, her pharmacy and her community health
worker — are absent for a different reason. They configure the client's own tracking application
so that it knows whom to tell when a reading comes back high; they are not facts about the client,
and the selection rule drops them as optional without this list naming them.

## No indicators

**This kit publishes no Annex C.** Its `input/indicators/` directory at the released tag holds
nothing but the `.gitignore` that would have hidden the workbook, and the implementation guide's
own indicators page is empty. So `ANNEXES` records no `C`, there is no `ANNEX_C` layout, and
[`../annex_c_coverage.py`](../annex_c_coverage.py) reports the kit as publishing none rather than
failing on the missing entry.

The consequence is in the selection rule. An element is carried when it is `Required = R`, or
feeds an Annex C indicator, or is named in the kit's core shortlist — and with no Annex C the
second clause reaches nothing at all. `Required = R` and the shortlist are the whole rule here,
which is why the shortlist does more work in this kit than in any other.

### The one row that looks indicator-linked

`SMBP.B4.DE.8 Contextual data for measurement required` is the single row in the dictionary whose
`Linkages to aggregate indicators` column is not blank. What it holds is `SMBP.B4.DT.01` — a
decision-support table id, in the column beside the one that names those.

The linkage rule cannot tell the difference and selects the row, which is the right outcome
reached the wrong way: that Boolean is the gate the kit's entire contextual block hangs off, and a
form without it asks nine questions about caffeine and posture with nothing to say when they
matter. So the element is named in `CORE_ELEMENTS` as well, and a WHO revision that tidies the
column changes nothing.

## The selection rule

`Required = R` carries the client's diagnosis, her conditions and medications, her prior-reading
source, the arm, the four baseline measurements with their timestamps, every reading on the second
form, and the danger signs. The shortlist carries four groups that rule leaves out.

### What a reading is read against

Height and current weight are `O`, and the body mass index computed from them is `C` on both.
All three are the risk stratification a raised reading in pregnancy is interpreted against, and
carrying the index alone would leave the kit's own calculation with no inputs.

The expected date of delivery is `O` and is the only element the kit gives gestational age from.
Before and after 20 weeks are different hypertensive disorders, so without it a reading cannot be
classified at all.

### The prior reading the mandatory question asks after

`SMBP.A9.DE.51 Pre-pregnancy BP readings` is `R` and asks which prior reading is available — from
before the pregnancy, from the first ANC contact, or none. The reading itself is three `C`
elements: the date and the two numbers. Carrying the question without them records that a prior
measurement exists and none of its values.

### The baseline the set-up process exists to establish

`SMBP.A9.DE.66` and `SMBP.A9.DE.71`, the baseline averages, are `C` on the four readings above
them, which are all `R`. Every later reading is read against these two numbers. A set-up form
collecting the four measurements and neither average records the measurements and not the
baseline.

### The contextual block

Nine elements of the reading form — the time of the last caffeinated drink, tobacco product and
exercise, whether the bladder was empty, where the measurement was taken, how hot and how noisy
that place was, what the client was doing, and how she was sitting — are `O` or `C` and linked to
nothing. Caffeine, tobacco, exercise, a full bladder, the room and the posture all move a blood
pressure reading, which is why the kit asks after them the moment one comes back high. Left to the
first two rules the form carries the gate and none of what it gates.

## What the reader had to learn

Nothing. This is the first kit to add no rule to
[`../dak_generator.py`](../dak_generator.py): its header is a single row at the top of each tab,
its `Optionality` column is spelled the way the SRV kit spells it, its `Quantity sub-type` is
hyphenated the way most kits hyphenate it, and its `List - select one` / `List - select all that
apply` / `List value` types are written with the plain hyphen the SRV kit uses rather than the BDS
kit's en dash. Every one of those already had a rule.

Like the SRV kit it has neither of the two columns most kits put their options in — no `Multiple
Choice Type`, no `Input Options` — so a row's choice comes from its data type and an option's
string is its label. Like the SRV and BDS kits it has a `List to include this data element in`
column, and here that column is advisory rather than load-bearing: this kit's value sets are flat,
so an option's parent is the row above it either way.

## Dictionary defects carried as found

### A follow-up typed as one of its parent's values

`SMBP.A9.DE.49 Antihypertensive name` is typed `List value` in the `Current medications` list, and
its own description is "Name of antihypertensive medication the client currently uses". Read
literally it is an eighth medication a client can tick, and the string `Antihypertensive name`
lands in `survey_response_answers.body` as the drug she takes.

The kit states the intent twice over — in that description, and in an `Explain conditionality` of
`IF "Current medications" = "Antihypertensive"`, which is a follow-up question and not a value.
So `ELEMENT_TYPES` retypes the row `String` and the shortlist keeps it, giving a free-text
question after the medication list. The retype is in [`domain.py`](domain.py) rather than in the
generator so that the divergence from Annex A is visible and reviewable.

### A conditionality naming a label the dictionary does not define

The three prior-reading elements are conditional on `IF "Prior BP readings" ≠ "Prior BP readings
unavailable"`. There is no element labelled `Prior BP readings`: the question is
`SMBP.A9.DE.51 Pre-pregnancy BP readings`, and `Prior BP readings unavailable` is one of its
values.

Nothing is done about it, because nothing needs to be: `Explain conditionality` is prose in every
kit and the reader encodes none of it. It is recorded here because a reader checking why those
three questions are ungated will look for the answer.

### Five questions labelled `Time of measurement`

The set-up form carries five, `SMBP.A9.DE.63`, `DE.65`, `DE.68`, `DE.70` and `DE.72`, one after
each baseline measurement and one after the diastolic average. They are the kit's own labels and
they are kept, because the label is what a deployment matches on and a relabelling here would be a
sixth spelling of a question WHO named once. The codes carry the element id, so the five stay
distinct in the data.

### Two labels shared across the two tabs

`First seated systolic reading in arm for SMBP measurements` and its three siblings are on both
tabs — as `SMBP.A9.DE.62` … `DE.71` at set-up and `SMBP.B4.DE.2` … `DE.7` at each reading. They
are the same measurement taken at two moments, and the codes distinguish them, so both are
carried.

## Annex B: what the form expresses

Three decision tables, all reading the same two averages and the danger-sign list.

| Table | Rows | Trigger | Encoded |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SMBP.B4.DT.01` | 1 | systolic ≥ 140, diastolic ≥ 90 | yes — record the context |
| `SMBP.B4.DT.01` | 2–5 | systolic ≥ 130 / diastolic ≥ 80, **with diabetes** | no — see below |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.01` | 5–6 | systolic ≥ 160, diastolic ≥ 110 | yes, with a patient issue |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.01` | 9–10 | systolic ≥ 140, diastolic ≥ 90, **without diabetes** | yes, bounded below the severe band |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.01` | 7–8, 11–12 | the same action at a different threshold, **with diabetes** | no — see below |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.01` | 1–4 | blood pressure controlled | no — see below |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.02` | 1–8 | any of the eight danger signs | yes, with a patient issue |
| `SMBP.B7.DT.02` | 9 | `No symptoms` | no — see below |

### Ranges are bounded rather than open-ended

The kit's rows overlap: 165 mmHg satisfies both `≥ 160` and `≥ 140`. A decision table takes the
first row that matches, and a form shows every prompt whose gate is met, so the lower band is
bounded above rather than written `≥ 140`. One reading raises one prompt.

**A range gate's `end` is exclusive.** Tamanu evaluates one with lodash's `inRange`, so the test
is `start <= value < end` (`@tamanu/utils/src/criteria.ts`). A band is therefore written to the
value the next band *starts* at — `{start: 140, end: 160}` beneath `{start: 160, end: 1000}` — and
the two are contiguous. Written to its own last value instead, `{start: 140, end: 159}` covers
140–158 and a systolic of exactly 159 falls between the bands and raises nothing at all.

### A threshold that reads another survey

Four of `SMBP.B4.DT.01`'s five rows and six of `SMBP.B7.DT.01`'s twelve set a different threshold
for a client with diabetes — 130/80 rather than 140/90 for the contextual prompt, and 130/80 for
the referral `SMBP.B7.DT.01` gives a client without diabetes at 140/90. Every one of them reads
`Current health conditions`, which is `SMBP.A9.DE.37` — a question of the **set-up** form,
answered once when the client is enrolled.

A visibility gate names questions of the survey it is on. There is no way to write "systolic ≥ 130
and the client has diabetes" on the reading form, because the second half of that is not on it.
So the encoded thresholds are the ones that hold for every client, and a client with diabetes is
prompted at 140/90 rather than at hers.

What that costs is recoverable rather than lost. The registry carries her diabetes as a condition
on the registration every reading is recorded against, so a `metric__` layer over the two forms
can apply the threshold the form could not. That is why the registry's condition list runs past
the four hypertensive disorders — see the [README](README.md) § Registry conditions.

### No reassurance is encoded

Both of the kit's reassuring outputs are left out, for the same reason arrived at two different
ways: each can render beside the warning it contradicts.

`SMBP.B7.DT.01`'s first four rows tell a client her blood pressure is controlled and she should
continue monitoring. Each reads `Current health conditions`, which is the unreachable gate above —
and here the consequence of firing anyway runs the wrong way: a client with diabetes and a systolic
of 135 would be told her reading is fine, where the kit calls it high and sends her to a health
worker.

`SMBP.B7.DT.02`'s ninth row maps `No symptoms` to "continue to self-monitor regularly", and its
gate *is* reachable — `Danger signs` is on this form. What is not reachable is the kit's guard
against answering it with `No symptoms` **and** a real sign: that guard is a `Validation condition`
on `SMBP.B4.DE.41`, "Cannot select with any other option", and a validation condition is prose the
reader encodes no more than it encodes `Explain conditionality`. The generated question carries the
mandatory flag and no exclusivity rule. Tamanu matches a multi-select gate on any selected value,
so an answer of `["No symptoms", "Severe headache"]` satisfies the danger-sign gate and the
reassurance gate alike, and the form would show an urgent referral and a "keep monitoring" beside
it.

An unencodable warning under-triggers; an unencodable reassurance misinforms. So the reading form
says nothing when a reading is normal and nothing when no danger sign is reported.

### A value the decision logic names and the dictionary does not

`SMBP.B7.DT.01` reads `Current health conditions has Diabetes, pre-existing type 1` and the same
for `type 2`. The dictionary's `Current health conditions` list has neither: it offers
`Diabetes arising in pregnancy (gestational diabetes)`, `Diabetes, other or unspecified`, and
nothing between.

The form carries the dictionary's list, unaltered — an option string is what lands in
`survey_response_answers.body`, and inventing two would make the form disagree with Annex A. The
registry carries all four, because a registry condition is where a fact the kit's own logic reads
belongs when no form question states it.

### One danger sign no table classifies

`Severe non-abdominal pain` is an option of `SMBP.B4.DE.40 Danger signs` and appears in no row of
`SMBP.B7.DT.02`, which classifies the other eight. It raises no prompt. A rule for it would be a
clinical recommendation WHO has not made.

## Guidance is addressed to the client

This kit is a self-care intervention, and its Annex B output column is headed **"Guidance
displayed to client"**: `Your blood pressure is very high. You need to go to a health facility
right now to help protect you and your baby.`

Tamanu's form is filled in at a facility or by a health worker reviewing what the client
submitted, so the prompts restate the same recommendation for whoever is at the form — *the client
needs to go to a health facility right now.* The recommendation is the annexe's; the addressee is
the form's.

## What cannot be encoded

| | |
|---|---|
| The diabetes-lowered thresholds | The condition is on the other survey. See § A threshold that reads another survey |
| Either reassurance | Each can render beside the warning it contradicts. See § No reassurance is encoded |
| `Validation condition` | Prose, like `Explain conditionality`. `No symptoms` and the two "no medications" options are marked "Cannot select with any other option" and the form does not enforce it |
| `Explain conditionality` | Prose in every kit, encoded in none. The contextual block's nine questions are asked whether or not `Contextual data for measurement required` is `Yes` |
| The three calculations | Body mass index and the two baseline averages state a formula in Annex A's `Calculation` column. They are carried as numbers for the operator to enter, not as Tamanu calculated questions, because a `math.js` calculation cannot reference a code containing hyphens and every question code here has them |
| Annex D | Functional and non-functional requirements — a conformance checklist for the deployment, not form config |

## Fetching the components

The annexes are not committed. [`domain.py`](domain.py) records the URL and, for the two that are
read, the sha256 of the copy these artefacts were generated from, so a WHO revision announces
itself rather than quietly changing a form:

```bash
mkdir -p forms/who-dak/smbp/annexes
curl -sL -o "forms/who-dak/smbp/annexes/SMBP DAK_core data dictionary.xlsx" \
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-dak-smbp/v1.0.0/input/dictionary/SMBP%20DAK_core%20data%20dictionary.xlsx
curl -sL -o "forms/who-dak/smbp/annexes/SMBP DAK_decision-support logic.xlsx" \
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-dak-smbp/v1.0.0/input/decision-logic/SMBP%20DAK_decision-support%20logic.xlsx
```

The generator prints the first of those when the file is absent.
