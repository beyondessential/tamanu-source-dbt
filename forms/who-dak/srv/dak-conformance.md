# DAK conformance — infectious disease surveillance

What these forms take from the WHO Surveillance DAK, what they deliberately do differently, and
what the DAK specifies that a Tamanu form cannot hold.

## Source

Everything is generated by [`../dak_generator.py`](../dak_generator.py) from the kit's **core data
dictionary** (`SRV DAK_core data dictionary.xlsx`, 599 labelled rows across six clinical tabs of
the eleven the workbook holds). It gives each data element an ID (`CHOLSRV.C1.DE.12`), a label, a
data type, an `Optionality` flag, terminology codes (ICD-11 / ICD-10 / LOINC / SNOMED CT / ICHI /
ICF / ATC) and — for coded elements — one child row per input option.

Fifteen further questions come from the **decision-support logic**. Nothing is authored by hand.

**This kit is not published as IRIS Web Annexes.** Its own README says the publication link is
"forthcoming", and WHO ships the four components through its SMART guidelines implementation guide
at [smart.who.int/dak-srv](https://smart.who.int/dak-srv/). The component pages there serve
whatever the site last built, so [`domain.py`](domain.py) pins each component to WHO's own source
repository for that guide — `WorldHealthOrganization/smart-dak-srv` — at the **`v1.0.0` tag**,
whose `sushi-config.yaml` declares `status: active` and `releaseLabel: release`. That is a
version-pinned path to the released bytes, which the component page is not; the component page is
recorded alongside it as `page`.

## What the forms are

Fourteen surveys: 104 dictionary questions across 41 screens, and 15 more from Annex B. Four of
the Annex B questions are patient issues, which Tamanu counts as answerable and the other eleven
are instructions, so the spec holds 108 answerable questions of 160 rows in all.

Three per disease — notification, laboratory, investigation — plus two disease-agnostic surveys
that bracket them. See the [README](README.md) for the table.

## A tab is a disease

This is the kit's structural signature, and it is a shape no other kit has.

Every other DAK gives each business process a tab of its own and names the process in the tab's
title: `HIV.D Care-Treatment`, `CHE.B7-9.LTI-DangerSigns`, `TB.C.Diagnosis`. The Surveillance kit
gives each **disease** a tab — `Cholera`, `Measles`, `Bacterial Meningitis`, `Yellow fever` — and
runs all five of its processes down every one of them, carrying the process only in the activity
id:

| Activity | Process |
|---|---|
| `CHOLSRV.C1`, `C6`, `C8`, `C10`, `C12`, `C13` | the clinician: evaluate, apply the case definition, take the specimen, record the outcome |
| `CHOLSRV.D1`, `D8`, `D9` | the laboratory: register the specimen, encode the result |
| `CHOLSRV.E4`, `E6` | the surveillance officer: notify, settle the final classification |
| `CHOLSRV.F1`, `F5` | outbreak investigation |
| `MSLSRV.G1`, `G3`, `G4` | contact tracing |

A survey is therefore a **slice of a disease tab taken by process**, which is what `activities`
already expresses — the mechanism the IMMZ kit introduced for a kit packing several processes onto
one tab. Here it does the opposite job: splitting one tab five ways rather than gathering nine
activities into three.

Three surveys per disease rather than five. The kit's five processes are three moments with three
different people in them, and splitting further would leave forms of one and two questions —
meningitis has two elements under `F1`, yellow fever one under `E4`.

### The disease is in the element id

`elementPrefix` is empty, which no other domain does. This kit names itself six ways — `SRVREG.`,
`SRV.`, `CHOLSRV.`, `MSLSRV.`, `MENSRV.`, `YFSRV.` — and the name is what makes an id unique:
`CHOLSRV.C1.DE.12` is the cholera case definition and `MSLSRV.C1.DE.12` is a measles symptom.
Stripping the prefix, as every other domain does, would collide the two onto one question code and
silently drop the second.

So a question code keeps the id whole: `whodaksrv-cholsrv-c1-de-12`.

## Three grains

The kit mixes three grains on one tab, and only one of them is a form.

| Grain | Where | Carried? |
|---|---|---|
| **The case** — what this client presented with, what the laboratory found, how it was classified | `C`, `D`, `E4`, `E6`, `F1`, `G` | yes |
| **The aggregate report** — what a surveillance unit sends upward for a period | `E7` | no |
| **The outbreak** — whether one is under way and how it is trending | `E9`, cholera's `F5` | no |

`E7` is "Report to admin 0 and/or 1 authorities", and every element under it on all four diseases
is a count or a population figure: `Number of suspected cholera cases`, `Number of lab confirmed
cases`, `Number of suspected cases tested`, `Doubling of cases in 3 weeks`, `Population size`,
`Number of suspected cases per week`. Not one is an answer a health worker gives about the client
in front of them.

A form that asked for them would ask a health worker to count records the deployment already
holds, and would store a number that disagrees with those records the moment one more case is
notified. What `E7` states is what a `metric__` layer computes from the case records — the
aggregation belongs downstream, which is where this repository's architecture puts it.

`E9 Outbreak presence?` and cholera's `F5 Outbreak trends` are the same argument at the outbreak's
grain rather than the report's.

Those three activities are named in no survey's `activities` list, so leaving them out needs no
mechanism beyond the allowlist that was already there.

## What the patient record keeps

`Registration common` is the kit registering its own client, which it has to do because a DAK
cannot assume a record exists. Tamanu has one, so 26 of that tab's 28 elements are named in
`PATIENT_RECORD` and dropped:

name, identifier, date of birth (and its "unknown" flag), age, sex, address and its four
administrative levels, telephone, email, reporting facility and its code, the provider's name,
facility and telephone, the encounter date, the referral and its facility, id, reason and health
worker, and the admission type.

Collected onto a form as well, each would give a deployment two places to write one fact and no
rule for which counts — and the second place is the one no other part of Tamanu reads.

**Two are kept**, because they are clinical rather than administrative and the patient record has
no field for either:

- `SRVREG.B5.DE.37 Patient symptoms`
- `SRVREG.B5.DE.38 Date of onset of first symptom(s)`

The onset date is the one every incidence and timeliness indicator dates a case from, and only
measles and yellow fever restate it on their own tabs (`Date of rash onset`, `Date of fever
onset`), so dropping the whole tab would leave cholera and meningitis with no onset at all.

This is the CHE kit's registration decision applied one element at a time instead of one tab at a
time, and `PATIENT_RECORD` is the rule that makes it reviewable: the run prints every element it
dropped and why.

## What the reader had to learn

Six rules, each absorbed into [`../dak_generator.py`](../dak_generator.py) rather than forked
around.

### The header columns are starred

Fourteen of each tab's 47 columns end in an asterisk — `Data element label*`, `Optionality*`,
`Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) code*` — marking what the kit's own instruction tab calls
"the columns required to be filled in for every data element".

One of them is `Data element label`, which is how every kit's header row is identified. Left on,
nothing matches, no header row is found on any tab, and the kit cannot be read **at all** rather
than merely read wrong. `header_key` now strips a trailing asterisk.

### `Optionality` where every other kit writes `Required`

Same three values — `R`, `O`, `C` — under a different name, added as an alias.

### The choice is in the data type

This kit has **no multiple-choice column** and **no input-options column**. It states the choice in
the data type instead:

| This kit | Every other kit |
|---|---|
| `List - select one` | `Coding` + `Select one` |
| `List - select all that apply` | `Coding` + `Select all that apply` |
| `List value` | `Codes` + `Input Option` |

383 of the 599 rows are `List value`. The reader maps the three spellings and falls back to the
data-type cell for the choice where a kit has no column of its own.

`choice_type` also had to match the whole cell before splitting a qualifier off it: it splits on
`" - "` to get `Select one` out of `Select one - Check box`, and that same split turns
`List - select one` into `List`, leaving every list on the kit untyped.

With no options column, an option's string is its label — which is what makes the duplicate below
unrecoverable by the rule that already existed for it.

### The activity's id and name are separate columns

Every other kit writes both in one cell — `HIV.E1 Capture or update client history` — and the
screen heading is that cell with its leading id stripped off. This kit gives them a column each,
so reading the id alone heads a screen `MENSRV.C1` with no words on it. The two are joined on
read, and the rest of the reader sees the single cell it sees everywhere else.

### An empty prefix is still a prefix

An activity heading takes the DAK's own id for its code. That extraction was gated on the domain
declaring a prefix to strip, and this kit declares none — so the code became forty characters of
the activity's sentence, cut mid-word: `...-act-cholsrv-e6-review-case-classification-us`. The
gate now distinguishes "nothing to strip" from "no id here" (a section heading), and the code is
`...-act-cholsrv-e6`.

### Indicators on a sheet per disease

Annex C is split the way the dictionary is — a sheet per disease plus `SRVCommon` — so
`ANNEX_C["sheet"]` accepts a list, and `firstDataRow` is applied to each sheet rather than to the
run of them. Reading only the first would answer for the three disease-agnostic indicators and
call every disease's own a gap.

## Dictionary defects carried as found

### `List to include this data element in` means five things

The column is overloaded, and only one of its uses matches its name:

| Use | Rows |
|---|---|
| on an option row, naming its parent | 383 |
| on a parent, holding the whole option list newline-separated | 81 |
| on a Boolean, holding its implied yes/no | 25 |
| on a String, ID, Date, Quantity or DateTime, holding a UI hint — `Dropdown menu/search from country list` | 31 |

The Boolean use is spelled five ways: `Yes/No`, `True/False`, `Yes / No`, `Yes /No`, `Yes  /No`.

### The column names the parent wrongly on 13 rows

Because of that, an option's parent is resolved **positionally** — the nearest preceding parent
row, which is how every other kit's options attach — and the column is a cross-check rather than
the key. Every one of the 383 option rows resolves that way; 13 would not resolve by name:

- `MENSRV.C6.DE.51` is labelled `Pneumococcal vaccine received?`, but its three options say their
  parent is `Status of pneumococcal vaccine`. The same paraphrase happens for the Hib vaccine and
  for five `Status of ... test` lists on the laboratory tab.
- `MENSRV.C6.DE.60` is labelled `Formulation of the pneumoccocal vaccine`; two of its options name
  `Formulation of the pneumococcal vaccine`, spelled correctly, and a third repeats the parent's
  transposition.

### An option row can carry a different activity id than its parent

`SRV.C11.DE.1 Diagnosis` sits under activity `SRV.C11`; its five options are `SRV.C1.DE.2` to
`DE.6`, under `SRV.C1`. On the meningitis tab, `MENSRV.C2`, `C3`, `C4` and `C5` contain **nothing
but** option rows — five activity ids sharing one name, `Conduct clinical evaluation`, four of
them with no element of their own.

Harmless, because options attach to their parent rather than to an activity, and an activity with
no element opens no screen. Worth stating because it means the activity column cannot be trusted
on an option row.

### Two options labelled as another option of the same question

`MENSRV.C6.DE.37` and `DE.61` each carry the label of another option of the same list — `DE.46`
and `DE.62`, the conjugated formulations. Two identical options collapse to one and lose an
answer, and the rule that already recovers this — fall back to the row's own label — has nothing
to fall back on, because with no options column the label *is* the option.

The kit's own code mappings keep the two apart where its labels ran together, and both are the
**unconjugated** antigen labelled as the conjugated one:

| Element | ATC | ICD-11 | Substance |
|---|---|---|---|
| `MENSRV.C6.DE.37` | `J07AH01` | `XM37L5` | Meningococcus A, purified polysaccharides antigen |
| `MENSRV.C6.DE.46` | `J07AH10` | `XM2280` | …antigen **conjugated** |
| `MENSRV.C6.DE.61` | `J07AL01` | `XM91D7` | Pneumococcus, purified polysaccharides antigen |
| `MENSRV.C6.DE.62` | `J07AL02` | `XM96S7` | …antigen **conjugated** |

`OPTION_LABELS` relabels `DE.37` and `DE.61` to their ATC substance name — a string the kit
already gives the row, so nothing is invented. The run prints both.

Neither list is on a form as things stand: `Formulation of meningococcal vaccine` and
`Formulation of the pneumoccocal vaccine` are `Optionality = C` and no indicator names them, so
the selection rule leaves both out. The relabel is on the element rather than on the form, so the
lists carry their twelve and three distinct answers the moment a deployment selects either.

### Three option rows marked as nothing at all

`SRVREG.B5.DE.26`, `DE.27` and `DE.28` are the Yes / No / Unknown of `SRVREG.B5.DE.25 Referral`,
left with no data type and no `List value`. By rule that is indistinguishable from a section
heading, so `OPTION_ROWS` names them, as the TB kit's three unmarked options are named. They are
options of an element the patient record already holds, so naming them changes no form; unnamed,
they would be three headings standing over the questions after them.

### The meningitis tab inserts three columns mid-header

`Meningococcus`, `Hib` and `Pneumococcus` sit between `Description and definition*` and
`Data type*`, flagging which pathogen an element applies to — 50 columns where the other tabs have
46 or 47. Costless, because columns are matched on their header rather than their position, but it
is the reason they must be.

### Two template tabs shipped unremoved

`Workflow ID (with examples)` still holds the WHO template's ANC, TB and IMMZ example rows, under
the instruction "please remove this row and the examples below". `HealthDomainAbbv.Configuration`
still has the placeholder name the template tells its author to replace, and its rows are
`[Activity ID]` / `[Activity name]`.

Annex C carries four more: `Meningitis (old)`, and three marked `not used, merged in Mngts`.

Neither reaches a form, because `PROCESSES` and `ANNEX_C` name their tabs one at a time. A reader
that swept the workbook by pattern would build surveys out of another kit's examples.

### One activity written two ways, one written wrongly

`CHOLSRV.F5` is both `Compile and submit reports to regional authorities` and `Submit investigation
reports to admin 1 and/or 2 authorities`. `MENSRV.D8` is `Encode results in LMIS` where cholera,
measles and yellow fever all say `LIMS`.

Neither changes a form: `F5` is outbreak-grain and carried nowhere, and `D8`'s wording only heads a
screen.

## Decision support

Annex B states a case-definition table and an outbreak-threshold table per disease, and a second
case-definition table for meningitis — one routine, one for an outbreak — under the same rule id.

Fifteen questions are generated from the classification tables: after the health worker records a
classification, the guidance the annexe attaches to it is shown, in the annexe's own words, gated
on that answer. Every classification the annexe attaches guidance to and the form offers as an
answer has one. Four also raise a patient issue, for the three confirmed classifications and
laboratory-confirmed measles.

| Rule | Survey | Gated on | Guidance for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CHOLSRV.C2.DT` | `chol-case` | `CHOLSRV.C1.DE.12` | suspected, confirmed |
| `MSLSRV.E6.DT` | `msl-inv` | `MSLSRV.E6.DE.97` | laboratory-confirmed, epi-linked, clinically compatible, non-measles discarded |
| `MENSRV.C2.DT` | `men-inv` | `MENSRV.E6.DE.214` | suspected, probable, confirmed |
| `YFSRV.C2.DT` | `yf-case` | `YFSRV.C1.DE.24` | suspected, confirmed |

`CHOLSRV.C2.DT` states a third output, `Flag as not a suspected cholera case`, which the form has
no answer for: cholera's case-definition element offers `Suspected case` and `Confirmed case`
only, and the discard is recorded later at `E6`. A gate needs an option to fire on.

**The outbreak-threshold tables are not encodable.** They compare this week's case count against
last week's across a surveillance unit — an aggregate over cases, not a fact about the one in front
of the health worker, and a form has no access to it. What they state is a `metric__` layer's rule.

The classification tables themselves are also not *evaluated* by the form. Each is a multi-column
decision table — cholera's takes age, acute watery diarrhoea, dehydration level, outcome, cause of
death, culture, PCR and clustering — and a Tamanu form can gate a prompt on one answer, not run a
hit policy across eight. The health worker applies the case definition and records the result; the
form responds to what they recorded.

## Annex C coverage

39 indicators across the five live sheets. `../annex_c_coverage.py --domain srv`:

| | |
|---|---|
| covered by the forms | 1 |
| gap in the forms | 0 |
| computation not quoted | 38 |
| no computable numerator | 0 |

**Zero gaps, and almost nothing checkable.** This kit states its computations in prose rather than
quoting element labels: `COUNT of all new suspected and confirmed cases reported during the given
interval`, `COUNT of all cholera alerts investigated within 48 hours of notification`. Only
`CHOLSRV.IND.3` quotes — `COUNT of all specimens for which "Date of laboratory result" "Date of
specimen receipt at the laboratory" ≤ 4` — and both elements it names are on the forms.

That is the ANC kit's position, and it means the check can confirm this kit only one indicator
deep. What the other 38 count is nonetheless what the case surveys collect: cases by
classification, specimens by date, deaths by outcome. Establishing that is a `metric__` layer's
job against the real forms, not something Annex C's prose can be read to prove.

Note that many of those computations count exactly what `E7` asks a surveillance unit to type in.
That is the argument in § Three grains from the indicator side: the numbers are derivable, so they
should be derived.

## What cannot be encoded

- **Outbreak thresholds.** Aggregate over cases and over time. See § Decision support.
- **The aggregate report.** `E7`, and `E9` / `F5` at the outbreak's grain. See § Three grains.
- **The case-definition decision tables as logic.** A form gates a prompt on one answer, not a
  hit policy across eight columns. See § Decision support.
- **Contact tracing as its own record.** `MSLSRV.G1` asks whether *this case* had contact with
  another case, which is a fact about the case and is carried. A contact as a person of their own,
  with their own follow-up, is a second registration this program does not model.
- **Annex D**, the functional and non-functional requirements: a conformance checklist for the
  deployment, not form content.

## Fetching the components

The annexes are not committed. `dak_generator.py` prints the fetch command when one is absent, and
[`domain.py`](domain.py) records the sha256 of the three it reads, so a WHO revision announces
itself rather than quietly changing a form.

```bash
mkdir -p forms/who-dak/srv/annexes
curl -sL -o "forms/who-dak/srv/annexes/SRV DAK_core data dictionary.xlsx" \
    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/smart-dak-srv/v1.0.0/input/dictionary/SRV%20DAK_core%20data%20dictionary.xlsx
```
